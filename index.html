FOUNDATION

Understanding Crime, Law & Justice

The handout begins with the relationship between law and crime and explains the basic concepts used throughout the criminal justice system.

⚖️

What is Crime?

Crime is an act or omission punishable by public laws.

§

Law

Law is that which is laid down, established or decreed. Most laws are penal in nature and define crime, treat of its nature, and provide penalties.

∞

Nullum Crimen

Nullum crimen nulla poena sine lege means there is no crime where there is no law punishing it.

⚑

Justice

Justice is the act of rendering what are due and treating persons equally, with persons falling within the same classification.

FOUR SPECIFIC TERMS OF CRIME
A

Felonies

Violations of the Revised Penal Code.

B

Offenses

Violations of statutes or special laws.

C

Infraction or Misdemeanor

Violations of ordinances.

D

Delinquency

Violations committed by minor offenders.

HISTORICAL DEVELOPMENT

Criminal, Victim & Justice Concepts

These concepts describe how people and justice are referred to at different stages of the criminal justice system.

CRIMINAL

Terms Used in the Justice System

Criminological Sense

A person who committed a crime regardless whether it was reported or referred.

Legal Sense

A person who was found guilty beyond reasonable doubt.

Person of Interest

Used when there are several suspicious persons.

Suspect

Used when there is one suspected person left.

Respondent

Used when the matter is within the level of the prosecution.

VICTIM

Victimology

Victimology is the scientific study of the extent, nature and causes of criminal victimization, its consequences for persons involved, and society's reactions thereto.

01 Primary Victim 02 Secondary Victim 03 Tertiary Victim
VICTIMLESS CRIMES

Crimes against the law without a private offended party

These are crimes against the law that only harm the individual who consented to engage in them. There is no private offended party in these crimes.

Gambling Prostitution Possession of Firearms Drug-related Cases

PRINCIPLES

Justice & Due Process

The handout connects justice with due process and explains three dimensions of justice.

01

Procedural Justice

Focuses on the fairness of the processes by which decisions are made.

02

Distributive Justice

Pertains to the fair allocation of resources and penalties.

03

Restorative Justice

Emphasizes repairing harm caused by criminal behavior and fostering reconciliation between offenders and victims.

DUE PROCESS

Two Kinds of Due Process

Procedural Due Process

Ensures that the procedures and methods used by government before depriving a person of life, liberty or property are fair and just.

Essential elements: Notice and Hearing
Substantive Due Process

Protects individuals from unreasonable, oppressive, or arbitrary laws, regardless of the procedure used to enforce them.

§

1987 Philippine Constitution

Under Sec. 1, Art. III, no person shall be deprived of life, liberty or property without due process of law, nor shall a person be deprived of the equal protection of law.

LAW ENFORCEMENT

Arrest & Search

Explore the arrest concepts and search-and-seizure information contained in the handout.

ARREST

Taking a person into custody of the law

Arrest is a taking of a person into custody of the law so that the person may be bound to answer for the commission of an offense.

Actual restraint / submission to custody Reasonable force At any time of day and night Bring the person to the nearest police station/jail

Three Types of Arrest

01

Arrest by an officer with a warrant

02

Arrest by an officer without a warrant

03

Arrest by a private person or "citizen's arrest"

Warrant of Arrest

A warrant of arrest is a formal legal document issued by a judge that authorizes law enforcement officers to arrest a specific individual accused of committing a crime.

Requisites for Issuing a Warrant

  1. Probable Cause
  2. Filing of Complaint or Information
  3. Judicial Review
  4. Specificity of the Warrant
  5. Execution of the Warrant
Life Span of Warrant

As long as the person stated in the warrant has not been arrested, the warrant remains valid even if several years have passed. The handout also states that the head of the office receiving the warrant for execution shall cause it to be executed within ten (10) days from receipt.

Valid Warrantless Arrest

A peace officer effecting an arrest without a warrant may be considered valid when it falls within the circumstances identified in the handout.

01 In Flagrante Delicto
02 Hot Pursuit
03 Arrest of an Escapee
Information to the person arrested

The arresting officer shall inform the person of the authority to arrest and the cause of the arrest, plus the Miranda Warning.

Search & Seizure

Search refers to examination of an individual's person, house, papers or effects, or other buildings and premises to discover contraband or evidence of guilt to be used in the prosecution of a criminal action.

Seizure is the taking of the property after being examined.

Search Warrant

A search warrant is an order in writing issued in the name of the People of the Philippines, directed to a peace officer, commanding him to search for personal property described therein and bring it before the court.

10 DAYS Validity period

Personal Property to be Seized

  • Subject of the offense
  • Stolen or embezzled property and proceeds or fruits of the offense
  • Property used or intended to be used as the means of committing the offense

Circumstances of Warrantless Search

Warrantless search incidental to lawful arrest Evidence in plain view Search of a moving vehicle Consented warrantless search Customs search Stop and frisk search Exigent and emergency circumstances

Instigation vs Entrapment

INSTIGATION

Exists when a police officer induces a person to commit a crime. Without the inducement, the crime would not be committed.

Example identified in the handout: sell-bust operation.
ENTRAPMENT

Refers to ways and means devised by the arresting officer to catch or apprehend a person who has committed a crime.

Example identified in the handout: buy-bust operation.

EVOLUTION OF LAW

Major Legal Developments

The handout presents several historical bodies and sources of law.

01

Code of Hammurabi

The first known bodies of law, promulgated by King Hammurabi of ancient Babylon and engraved on stone tablets. It dealt with theft, ownership, sexual relationships and violence.

02

Roman Law

Derived from the Twelve Tables, a collection of basic rules related to family, religion and economic life.

03

Common Law

Traced from early English society and described as a body of early unwritten legal principles created from social customs, rules and practices.

04

The Magna Carta

Signed on June 15, 1215 by King John of England. It guarantees basic liberties for all British citizens.

05

Greek Laws

Contributed to Western thoughts about law through ideas associated with philosophers such as Socrates, Plato and Aristotle.

06

Canon Law

Also known as Ecclesiastical Law and adopted for the government of the Christian organization and its members.

07

Germanic Law

A family of customary laws from the Germanic tribes.

08

Philippine Laws

For early Filipinos, laws were both written and unwritten. Written laws were decreed and codified, while unwritten laws were based on customs and traditions.

09

Maragtas Code

Described in the handout as a written code in the pre-Spanish era and the oldest body of laws in the Philippines. It states that it was written by Datu Sumakwel of Panay in 1250 A.D.

10

Code of Kalantiaw

The handout identifies it as the second oldest legal code in the Philippines and states that it was written by Datu Kalantiaw of Panay in 1433 A.D.

07

LEGAL FOUNDATION

Basis of the Philippine Criminal Justice System

The handout states that the Philippine Criminal Justice System is based on enacted Philippine criminal law.

§

Criminal Law

Criminal law is a branch of public law which defines crime, treats of their nature and provides for their punishment.

CONSTITUTION

1987 Philippine Constitution

Article XVI, Section 6 provides that the state shall establish and maintain one police force which shall be national in scope and civilian in character, administered and controlled by a National Police Commission (NAPOLCOM).

RA 4864

Police Act of 1966

Enacted on September 8, 1966 and identified in the handout as creating NAPOLCOM to oversee the police function.

PD 765

Integrated National Police

Enacted on August 8, 1975 and established the Integrated National Police composed of the Philippine Constabulary as the national police and the integrated national police as local police.

RA 6975

DILG Act of 1990

Identified as the Department of Interior and Local Government Act of 1990, unifying the local and national police into the Philippine National Police.

RA 8551

PNP Reform & Reorganization Act

Identified in the handout as the Philippine National Police Reform and Reorganization Act of 1998.
