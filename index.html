01 • Core concepts

Crime, law, justice & victims

Use the cards as quick-review notes before moving to the system structure.

⚖

What is Crime?

Crime is an act or omission punishable by public laws.

  • Felonies: violations of the Revised Penal Code.
  • Offenses: violations of statutes or Special Laws.
  • Infraction/Misdemeanor: violations of ordinances.
  • Delinquency: violations committed by minor offenders.
§

Law before crime

Nullum crimen nulla poena sine lege means there is no crime where there is no law punishing it.

Crime is a product of the law.
≡

Justice

Justice is the act of rendering what are due and treating persons equally, with persons falling within the same classification.

Due process is required for justice to be realized.
◉

Victim

Victimology is the scientific study of criminal victimization, its consequences, and society's reactions to it.

Primary Secondary Tertiary
A

Three dimensions of justice

1
Procedural Justice

Focuses on fairness of the processes by which decisions are made.

2
Distributive Justice

Pertains to the fair allocation of resources and penalties.

3
Restorative Justice

Emphasizes repairing harm and fostering reconciliation among offenders, victims, and the community.

B

Due process

Procedural due process

Ensures procedures and methods used by government before depriving a person of life, liberty, or property are fair and just.

Notice Hearing
Substantive due process

Protects individuals from unreasonable, oppressive, or arbitrary laws, regardless of the procedure used to enforce them.

Victimless crimes

Crimes against the law with no private offended party

The handout describes these as crimes against the law that only harm the individual who consented to engage in them.

Evolution of law

Historical legal foundations

Code of Hammurabi

First known bodies of law; promulgated by King Hammurabi of ancient Babylon and engraved on stone tablets.

Roman Law

Derived from the Twelve Tables, a collection of basic rules related to family, religion and economic life.

Common Law

Traced from early English society; early unwritten legal principles created from social customs, rules and practices.

The Magna Carta

Signed June 15, 1215 by King John of England; the handout states it guarantees basic liberties for British citizens.

Greek Laws

Contributed to Western thoughts about law through ideas of philosophers like Socrates, Plato and Aristotle.

Canon Law

Also known as Ecclesiastical Law; adopted for the government of the Christian organization and its members.

Germanic Law

A family of customary laws from the Germanic tribes.

Philippine Laws

For early Filipinos, laws were both written and unwritten. Written laws were decreed and codified; unwritten laws were based on customs and traditions.

Maragtas Code

A written code in the pre-Spanish era; the handout identifies it as the oldest body of laws in the Philippines and says it was written by Datu Sumakwel of Panay in 1250 A.D.

04 • Law enforcement

Arrest, warrants & search

Use the tabs to study the rules presented in the handouts.

01

Officer with a warrant

Arrest by an officer with a warrant.

02

Officer without a warrant

Valid warrantless arrest may occur in the listed circumstances.

03

Private person / citizen's arrest

Arrest by a private person or “citizen's arrest” without a warrant.

How an arrest should be made

  1. By actual restraint/submission to the custody of the person making the arrest.
  2. Reasonable force.

When: At any time of the day and night.

After arrest: Bring the person to the nearest police station/jail.

Valid warrantless arrest

  1. In flagrante delicto arrest
  2. Hot pursuit arrest
  3. Arrest of an escapee

Officer must inform the person

  1. Authority to arrest
  2. Cause of the arrest
  3. Miranda Warning / Miranda Rights
W

Warrant of Arrest

A formal legal document issued by a judge that authorizes law enforcement officers to arrest a specific individual accused of committing a crime. It is an order in writing issued in the name of the People of the Philippines, signed by the judge and directed to a peace officer.

Requisites for issuing a warrant of arrest

01 Probable Cause Rule 113, Rules of Court
02 Filing of Complaint or Information Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure, Rule 112
03 Judicial Review Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure, Rule 113
04 Specificity of the Warrant Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure, Rule 113
05 Execution of the Warrant Philippine National Police Operational Procedures
Life span: As long as the person stated in the warrant has not been arrested, the handout states that it remains valid even if several years have lapsed. The head of the office to whom it was delivered shall cause execution within ten (10) days from receipt.
⌕

Search & Seizure

Search refers to examination of an individual's person, house, papers or effects, or other buildings and premises to discover contraband or evidence of guilt to be used in prosecution of a criminal action. Seizure is the taking of the property after being examined.

Search warrant

  • Order in writing issued in the name of the People of the Philippines.
  • Signed by a judge and directed to a peace officer.
  • Commands a search for personal property described therein and to bring it before the court.
  • Validity period: ten (10) days.
  • Can be served only once within its validity period.

Property that may be seized

  • Subject of the offense.
  • Stolen or embezzled and other proceeds/fruits of the offense.
  • Used or intended to be used as the means of the commission of the offense.

Circumstances of warrantless search

Incidental to lawful arrest Plain view Moving vehicle Consented search Customs search Stop and frisk Exigent & emergency circumstances
Instigation

Police officer induces a person to commit a crime that would not otherwise be committed; the handout says this is not valid.

Entrapment

Means devised by the arresting officer to catch or apprehend a person who has committed a crime; the criminal mind originates from the accused himself.

Philippine National Police

First line of defense; mostly coordinated by government agencies in implementation of laws; under DILG.

National Bureau of Investigation

Highly trained investigative body, functionally integrated and national in scope; under DOJ.

Philippine Coast Guard

Vanguard against entry of unsafe foreign vessels; attached to DOTR, and in times of war becomes attached to DND.

Philippine Center on Transnational Crime

Formulates and implements a concerted program of action for prevention and control of transnational crime; under direct supervision of the Office of the President.

Department of Environment and Natural Resources

Tasked with enforcement of environmental laws of the Philippines.

Bureau of Immigration

Enforces immigration laws and ensures foreigners comply with existing laws.

Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency

Primarily concerned with enforcement of drug laws; under the Office of the President.

Legal foundation of the PNP
1987 Philippine Constitution, Art. XVI, Sec. 6

The state shall establish and maintain one police force, national in scope and civilian in character, administered and controlled by NAPOLCOM.

RA 4864

Police Act of 1966; created NAPOLCOM to oversee the police function.

PD 765

Enacted August 8, 1975; established the Integrated National Police.

RA 6975

Department of Interior and Local Government Act of 1990; unified local and national police into the PNP.

RA 8551

Philippine National Police Reform and Reorganization Act of 1998.
